# Hook data and pattern library

Source: MagicPost analysis of 1,179,958 LinkedIn posts (12 months, June 2026
pull, medians throughout so viral outliers can't tilt cells). The data is
about first lines in a feed, but the mechanism — a sub-second decision to
keep reading/watching — is the same one a video's first seconds face.

## The three findings

**1. Question openers lose.** Opens-with-a-question: 19 median likes, 4
comments. No question: 29 likes, 6 comments. A 34% penalty — and it holds in
every follower band (under 5k: 10 vs 13; 5k–50k: 26 vs 34; 50k+: 129 vs 187),
so it's a property of how audiences read openers, not of who's posting.
Hypothesized why: a question is the cheapest opener (reads as filler), it
pattern-matches to engagement bait, and it lets the viewer silently answer
"no" and scroll. A statement gives no exit.

**2. Numbers win.** First line contains a digit: 35 median likes vs 26
without. A number is a promise the content is concrete, countable, real.
Vagueness reads as filler; a number reads as proof.

**3. Short wins.** 1–5 words: 30 median likes. 6–10 words: 29. 11–15: 25.
16+: 28 (usually narrative cold-opens). The first line is a door, not the
room — make it small enough to walk through.

**The rule:** a short, specific statement beats a long, vague question.

## Pattern library (adapt, don't copy)

Rewrite these in the product's own numbers and the user's voice. Strongest
patterns first.

### Number & result (the overperformers)
- The data point: "We tested three strategies. One had three hundred percent more impact."
- The statistic: "Seventy percent of teams never see this coming. The other thirty percent do three things."
- The fast-track: "It took us three years to learn this. You get it in ninety seconds."
- The case study: "One change. Reply rate went from four percent to nineteen."
- The how-to: "Five fixes. Five minutes. Watch."

### Contrarian & myth-busting (statements with friction)
- The challenge: "Traditional dashboards are dead."
- The myth-buster: "Everyone says you need more data. You need less."
- The unpopular take: "Your pipeline is fine. The offer underneath it is losing."
- The reframe: "You don't have a downtime problem. You have a visibility problem."

### Story & confession (cold-open mid-scene)
- The cold open: "My hands were shaking. I read the alert three times."
- The confession: "We lost the account on a Tuesday. Best thing that happened to the product."
- The personal win: "Nine months of building. Here's what almost killed it."
- The turning point: "A year ago this plant flew blind. One change fixed it."

### Data point & trend
- The trend: "AI just changed field operations for good. Most teams still run it like it's twenty twenty-one."
- The behind-the-scenes: "We ship every six weeks. The process fits on one screen."
- The resource: "This one view cut triage time in half."
- The announcement: "Three years of building. It's finally live."

### Question hooks — use sparingly (the 34% underperformers)
Only when the tension is sharp and specific, and the payoff is immediate:
- "Ever sent an alert nobody read?" — only if the pain is universal and the next line answers it.
- "What if you saw the failure two hours before it happened?" — only if the video proves it on screen.
- Never a generic "Thoughts?"-style opener, and never "Ever struggle with X?"

## Beyond the hook

The hook buys the read; the body keeps it. The same specificity rules apply
to every re-hook inside the script: each new beat's first line should pass
the same test — short, specific, a number or a turn, almost never a question.
