# Manuka voice & positioning

Every script is a Manuka story first and a product story second. The goal is
not to sell the app on screen — it's to give customers a reason to engage
with Manuka: showcase real expertise, take a position, sound like people who
have actually built this. The viewer should finish thinking "these people
have done this before," not "that was a nice ad."

## The three angles

Every piece should land at least one of:

1. **Why customers work with us** — shown through the work, never claimed.
   The demo on screen is the evidence; the script never says "trusted
   partner" or lists credentials.
2. **What makes us different** — we ship end-to-end on the Databricks-native
   stack (Lakebase, Unity Catalog, Databricks Apps, agents on governed
   data), and the apps in this repo are the proof.
3. **Our perspective on the platform** — opinionated takes on Unity Catalog,
   AI Gateway, AI Observability, CDP, Omnigent, and other new Databricks
   releases. A sharp "here's what everyone gets wrong about X" beats a
   feature recap every time.

## Opinions are the point — but they're Manas's opinions

- Propose a take grounded in what the repo actually demonstrates (e.g.
  "chatbots don't fix supply chains; agents on governed data do") and flag
  it clearly for approval at the script-review step.
- For releases newer than your knowledge (e.g. Omnigent) or where the take
  isn't obvious from the code: ask for his position, or research first.
  Never bluff specifics about a release, and never invent a Manuka opinion
  on something the user hasn't weighed in on if the take is load-bearing.

## Tone

Natural, opinionated, practitioner-to-peer. First person plural ("we",
"our take"). Contractions are good. Short declarative sentences. It should
read like someone who builds these systems talking over coffee, not a brand
account.

**Banned:** marketing superlatives (seamless, game-changing, revolutionary,
cutting-edge, best-in-class, unlock, empower, leverage, supercharge),
"in today's fast-paced world"-style throat clearing, "we're excited to
announce", feature-tour phrasing, and stacked rhetorical questions.

**CTA:** close with an invitation to engage — "talk to us", "tell us where
we're wrong", "if this sounds like your Tuesday, come argue with us" —
never a hard sell ("book a demo now", "limited slots", urgency language).

## Litmus test before delivering

Read the script and ask: if you stripped the product shots, would this still
work as an opinionated post from a practitioner? If it only works as an ad,
rewrite it.
